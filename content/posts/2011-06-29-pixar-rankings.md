Samuel is obsessed with anything with wheels, so he of course loves the original Cars movie, and has been eagerly waiting for Cars 2 for the last year. We saw this last week (and I'm sure will be seeing it again soon), and I'm surprised by the poor reception it has been getting. Sam's a big Pixar fan, so I've seen all the movies numerous times over the last few years.

A Bug's Life has always struck me as the weakest Pixar film. It's not terrible, but it could be any recent animated film - there's none of the Pixar magic that all of the other films have. None of the characters are particularly appealing, and the plot is nothing special. Samuel also finds this fairly mediocre.

Although it's one of the more recent films (the most recent non-sequel), Up wasn't that great either. The first sequence of the movie is great - it would have been a great short. The main story in the movie didn't capitvate me at all. Some aspects, like the talking dogs, seemed completely out of place, and weakened an already weak movie.

Toy Story 2 was the first Pixar sequel. Unfortunately, it appears that they've succombed to sequels, with Cars 2, Toy Story 3, a rumoured Toy Story 4, a sequel to Monsters Inc., and a rumoured Incredibles sequel. Pixar's strongest work is in their original stories, and I long admired that they didn't get caught in the sequel trap. It is especially disappointing given their one-movie-a-year schedule, since that means no original story in 2010, 2011, or 2012. Toy Story 2 is a typical sequel, telling much the same story as the first movie, but with some elements rearranged. In some ways it's better than Toy Story, but not enough to overcome the lack of originality.

I expected to like Thet Incredibles more than I did. In many ways, it's like A Bug's Life, in that it didn't present very interesting characters, but it is significantly better than that film. I'm not entirely sure what doesn't work for me with this one, but it only just holds Samuel's interest as well. I have no enthusiasm about a sequel at all.

Toy Story is hard to judge, because it holds a significant place in film history - Pixar has had a monumental effect on animated film, and Toy Story is the film that kicked it all off. If you ignore that aspect, I feel Toy Story is a good film, but not a truly great one. In some ways, I feel it has been dinimished by the long run of sequels, and would have stood better alone. The animation is also significantly behind even A Bug's Life, and it is difficult to ignore that when comparing to the later films.

Cars 2 is unique in that I have only seen it once so far. On first viewing, I put it in the middle of the pack. It's a stronger sequel than Toy Story 2, especially in that it tells a completely new story with the same characters, rather than retelling the same story. However, it is still a sequel, and it does feel a little like an extended version of one of the films seen in the credits of Cars (Pixar movies turned into Cars versions). I feel the movie could have been stronger if it was told using entirely new characters (not necessarily cars), rather than using the ones we already new. character development was the weakest part of the movie - I don't feel I know anything at all about Finn or Holly, let alone the villians of the piece. However, the action was good and the plot was fine, and it was enjoyable in the ways that Up and A Bug's Life was not.

I

The first time I saw Cars I wasn't particularly impressed. However, having seen it hundreds of times since, it has grown on me. The characters develop well, and the ending is extremely well done. Although the movie is a marketeer's dream, it doesn't feel like that is the aim, rather it's a tribute from someone that is passionate about cars, particularly older ones. The movie has a good soundtrack, and an excellent credit sequence.

Toy Story 3 was much stronger than I expected. Rather than simply repeating the story from Toy Story and Toy Story 2, we get a continuation of some of the themes with a new adventure. There are truly moving moments, as with Cars (but not Cars 2), and real development of characters. The action sequences, like Cars 2, are exceptionally well done, and the opening of the movie is spectacular.

I enjoyed Monsters Inc. a lot, although I'm not entirely sure why. The main characters are very likeable, although they don't grow much in the movie at all. The entire concept of the movie is Pixar at its best - sufficiently original to be refreshing, not about humans, and just plain interesting. The credits sequence is good (although not as good as Cars. I don't have any desire to see a sequel (or prequel), however - I left the movie satisfied with how the characters' story ended.

I enjoyed Ratatitte a lot on first viewing, but it hasn't held up as well on repeated visits. Samuel likes the movie, but he doesn't love it. The general plot line was again great Pixar - interesting and fresh, focusing on non-humans (the human parts of the film were the weakest). The characters are again likeable, and grow through the movie.

WALL-E is a great film. The concept is interesting, and the characters are extremely likeable, although only Eve really grows through the film. The first part of the movie, before there are humans, is exceptional - a truly enjoyable silent animated film. The second half is considerably weaker, with uninteresting human characters, although it's still good. The credits sequence is very dull - a style unfrotunately repeated with Cars 2.

Finding Nemo is absolutely Pixar's finest work, and I think the one that will stand the test of time best. There are extremely likeable characters, with Dory probably the most lovable Pixar character of all the films. Many characters grow throughout the film, and the storyline is interesting and sufficnetly complex, even though it's not particuarly original. The film shows how Pixar does its best work when using non-human characters to show human nature, and mixes great comedy with good drama. The film is enjoyably by young children (with a few very scary scenes) and also by adults, in the fashion of the best Disney movies. I hope they never go a Nemo sequel: the fm ends on a satisfying note, and I cannot see how a sequel would add anything to these characters. It is interesting that the film starts with a death, like Up, and manages to be as effective, but with considerably less time, and that part of the story never felt like it should be the story, unlike Up.
