This week continues from the previous one, covering installing Visual Studio SP1, Visual Studio SDK, and the IronPython Visual Studio support example, and also the MultiDoc code from Chapter 6, which completes the initial specification of the MultiDoc application.  No new notes, but a new lab exercise [PDF], and two new recommended readings: [yet another Spolsky article, about excellent customer service](http://www.joelonsoftware.com/articles/customerservice.html) (not specifically about something that we cover in this course, but good advice, and some of the students will no-doubt end up interacting with customer service, either as customer service representatives, managers of such, or double-job'ing like Spolsky did), and a [Jack W. Reeves post about "What is Software Design"](http://www.developerdotstar.com/mag/articles/reeves_design.html) (long, but often recommended, and although it's old it's still quite relevant).
Even though the content was split over two weeks, there wasn't enough time to type out the MultiDoc code (I'm wondering how well one of the auto-typing utilities used in presentations might work in a teaching environment).  I converted the nicely modular code from [IronPython in Action](http://ironpythoninaction.com) into a single file, added copious comments, and replaced the lambdas with named functions.  I walked through this in detail, including the parts that we'd already covered.  We'll see MultiDoc again briefly next week for testing, and hopefully again when we look at [Silverlight](http://silverlight.net/), but for the most part, this is the end of the MultiDoc road.
I didn't have a lab exercise scheduled for this week originally, but I had hoped that Chapter 6 would fit in a single week.  I wanted something reasonably simple that included using properties, so I reused an exercise I had from a couple of years ago.  The idea is basically that the students create a class that slots into an existing project, and then have a simple drawing application.
