I asked [Vodafone](http://www.vodafone.co.nz) this question:
> I have a mobile broadband plan at the moment, which I periodically use for tethered use (i.e. for Internet access on a computer via a bluetooth connection to a generic cellphone). That works fine.
> I also have an iPhone, where the SIM will normally reside. I can't use the iPhone for tethering (because the iPhone does not support that). The existing broadband plan I have does not work on the iPhone.
> What I want is a plan that will allow me to do both - when the SIM is in the iPhone, the iPhone data connection will work, and when the SIM is in the other phone, the data connection will work there.
> If I switch to one of the iPhone data plans, will this be the case? Or will the data stop working on the non-iPhone phone?
> Is there any iPhone data plan (only data - I have a YouChoose plan for everything else) that does not require a fixed-term contract? I have no problem paying a higher monthly charge, but I do not really want a fixed-term data contract (although I would do that if there was no other option).

In my opinion, this is a pretty straightforward question, clearly worded, with sufficient background to answer the question.  I asked this on the 6th of October 2008 at 5:26 PM.  I received this response, on the 16th of October 2008 at 4:05 PM.
> Dear Tony,
> Thank you for your email.
> In regards to your query - what would be the easiest way to do this is to add on a IPHONE DATA plan. You can get either a 200MB or 1GB plan. These are both on 24month terms, you would need to visit a Vodafone store to get sign up for this.
> If you have any further questions please reply to this email, call our Customer Contact Centre on 777 from your Vodafone mobile, or 0800 800 021 from any other phone. From overseas you can contact us on + 64 9 355 2007.
> Yours sincerely
> Carol T
> Vodafone Customer Support

Vodafone, if you are listening (and Paul Brislen [appears to have a pretty efficient ego-filter](http://tonyandrewmeyer.wordpress.com/2008/08/29/vodafone-subsumes-ihug-cant-handle-internet-or-phone/)), here are some free tips about how you can improve the way you answer questions (in order of importance):

- Answer promptly.  This is especially important for sales queries (like this one), since you're likely to lose the customer to the speedier opposition if you don't.  10 days (8 working days) is not promptly; it's not even slowly, it's glacial.
- Answer the question asked, not some other question that you have a convenient answer to.  I did not ask "what would be the easiest way to do this".  I asked two questions: (1) if I switch to an [iPhone](http://www.apple.com/iphone) data plan, will tethering on a non-iPhone cellphone continue to work, and (2) is there any iPhone plan that does not require a fixed-term contract?  "Carol T"'s answer implies that the answer to (1) is "yes", but when answering a question, you need to be explicit, not implicit.
- Check if your answer simply tells the customer something they already know.  It's pretty obvious from my email that I already know that getting an iPhone data plan will allow me to use data on my iPhone (amazing!).  Telling me this, and nothing else, does not help me, and simply gives me the impression that rather than reading my carefully worded email, you scanned it for keywords.  I could write an auto-answering 'bot that would give out this answer.
- When using trademarked (or soon to be trademarked) terms, use them correctly.  It's "iPhone", not "IPHONE".  Doing this gives the impression that you are giving form answers, rather than answers from true product knowledge.
- Use correct grammar.  In "These are both on 24month terms, you would need to visit a Vodafone store to get sign up for this", the comma should be a semicolon (or there should be some sort of conjunction or a new sentence).  "Sign" should also be "signed".  The first sentence in that paragraph scans pretty awkwardly, too.  If you want to get picky, then put a space between "24" and "month".
- Be consistent: the 'traditional' style is to include the comma after "Dear Tony" and "Yours sincerely".  The modern style is to leave out the comma.  You shouldn't do both in one message.

I hope these are of use, and that I can find the answers to my questions somewhere else.  (Why didn't I go into a store and ask?  Because I had hopes that people answering emails would have more knowledge than retail staff, who (and this is a widespread problem, not limited to Vodafone) generally have very poor product knowledge).
