> Podcasting's first professionally-produced original drama series, SHADOW FALLS tells the eerie tale of a remote Northeastern town that holds secrets some will kill to protect and others will die to expose.

[Shadow Falls @ PodShow.com](http://shadowfalls.podshow.com/)

"Shadow Falls" has been getting a lot of hype on the [DSC](http://www.podshow.com/shows/?mode=detail&episode_id=15261) recently (to be expected, since [Adam Curry](http://curry.com) was involved in production), but it's weird how both Adam and listeners are describing this as something new.

[Podiobooks.com](http://podiobooks.com) has been serving up serialised fiction via podcasts for some time now (they have a great range - check them out), not to mention [Escape Pod](http://escapepod.org/)'s short fiction (or more radio-show-style podcasts like [The Radio Adventures of Dr. Floyd](http://www.doctorfloyd.com/)).  [Scott Sigler](http://scottsigler.net) is on (finished?  I'm a bit behind) his third original podcast novel, which **is hosted by PodShow**.  In fact, I remember [Adam mentioning Infection on the DSC](http://mp3.dailysourcecode.podshow.com/DSC-316-2006-01-16q.mp3) (not just playing a promo, but raving about it).

It's nice to have another story, but that's all it is.

I'm not all that convinced about the "first professionally-produced" claim, either.  What does "professionally-produced" mean?  That it was done in a professional manner?  That someone was paid to do it?  Seems like Scott Sigler's podcasts are professionally produced, as are some of the podiobooks.com ones.  In fact, podiobooks.com offer [a service where they produce the podiobook for the author](http://podiobooks.com/authors/pbpro.php).  If that's not "professionally-produced", I don't know what is.

I'd put a comment about this on the DSC or "Shadow Falls" pages, but you have to log in to PodShow Plus to do that, and [I can't](http://tonyandrewmeyer.wordpress.com/2006/07/22/podshow-plus-three-strikes-theyre-out/).
